Get started

The first start

Choose what PoHo may read, accept the terms, then let PoHo find your AI work or pick your project folders yourself.

The first time PoHo opens, you choose what it may do, and then which folders it may look in. PoHo reads nothing on your computer until you accept.

1. Choose what PoHo may do

The Welcome to PoHo Base screen has a switch for each permission. All are on by default; turn off any you do not want.

  • Read my AI coding sessions: the chat history of Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, OpenCode and Antigravity on this computer, so PoHo can show where you stopped and continue those chats.
  • Take pictures of my apps’ windows: only a project’s own app while it is open, never editors, terminals or browsers.
  • Check my projects’ websites: loads each project’s public website to take a picture and see that it responds.
  • Tell AI agents where I stopped: new Claude Code and Cursor chats start knowing the project and what is left. This adds a small hook to their settings. See Connect your AI tools.
  • Start with Windows: PoHo opens quietly in the tray when you sign in. This switch appears only in the installed app; when you start from the download, turn it on later in Settings → General.

Then tick I have read and accept the Terms of Use and the Privacy Notice. Click either name to read it right there.

Click Accept and install (when you started from the download, see Install PoHo) or Accept and continue.

Tip: You can change these choices any time: the first three in Settings → Privacy & safety → What PoHo may do, briefings in Settings → AI agents, and Start with Windows in Settings → General.

2. Show PoHo your projects

Next, PoHo offers two ways in:

  • Find my AI work (recommended): PoHo reads only where Claude Code, Codex and other agents worked, and lists those folders. Your drives are not searched.
  • Choose folders: pick a project folder, or a folder that holds your projects, yourself.

With Find my AI work, you see “AI worked in N folders”. Check the ones PoHo should show, then click Add N projects. Only those folders are read. If no AI work is found, click Choose folders instead.

More on folders: Add your project folders.

3. You are on Home

PoHo now reads each project’s name, README and git status, and the top bar shows “Looking for projects” while it works. Home then shows the project you were last in and where you stopped.

Note: To see this screen again, open ⚙ → Welcome screen. It does not change your projects or folders.
