Safety and privacy

What PoHo never does

PoHo never deletes your files; it only forgets its own memory, and changes project files only when you click.

PoHo never deletes your files, and it has no button that does. When you remove something in PoHo, it only forgets its own memory; your folders stay exactly where they are.

Forgetting is not deleting

  • Archive project (project page → Setup): the project leaves your lists. The files stay where they are and you can bring it back any time with Restore from archive.
  • Remove from PoHo… (the project’s … menu): PoHo stops showing it on Home, in the lists and in search. It moves to Archived in Projects, with its notes and history. Its files on your disk are not touched.
  • Stop watching this folder (Settings → Projects & folders): PoHo forgets the projects in that folder. Files on disk are not touched.
  • Start over (Settings → Privacy & safety): PoHo forgets its project list, notes, session memory and settings and opens at the welcome screen. A copy of its memory is kept in a backup folder.
  • Uninstall PoHo Base: removes the app, its shortcuts, start-with-Windows, its hooks and PoHo as a tool in your AI agents. Your project files are never touched. See Questions people ask.

Changes it makes only when you click

Apart from snapshots, which it keeps hidden in the project’s .git folder, PoHo touches a project’s files only when you ask it to:

  • adding notes for your AI to CLAUDE.md and AGENTS.md;
  • adding .gitignore lines for .env files;
  • putting a project on GitHub (adds a .gitignore and a first commit, then pushes to your own account);
  • making or merging a lane for another Claude account (a .lanes folder with its own branch);
  • restoring files from a snapshot.

It also changes another tool’s settings only when you click: connecting Claude Code, Cursor or Codex in Settings → AI agents, and Ask Claude Code to keep them too (keeps conversations for ten years instead of 30 days).

Restoring is safe too

Before PoHo puts a file back from a snapshot, it saves the current state first. If it can’t, nothing is restored. Files that are new since the snapshot are left alone. See Snapshots before AI sessions.

Tip: PoHo is not a backup tool. Keep your own backups.
